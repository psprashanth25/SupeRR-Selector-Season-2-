# Phase 7 — IPL 2022–2026 exploratory shortlist

## Scope
IPL competitions 2022–2026 only, 367 matches and 87,673 ball-by-ball records, excluding super-over innings. This is the most franchise-relevant view in the supplied dataset.

## Pressure methodology
Expected runs per legal ball estimated from IPL data by innings, phase (powerplay/middle/death), and wickets lost (0–2/3–5/6–9), with sparse cells smoothed toward phase/innings average. Batting pressure combines phase intensity, wickets lost and scoring below expected (first innings) / required rate above expected (chase). Bowling pressure combines phase, batting resources and scoring above expected. High pressure >=65.

## Batter eligibility
At least 100 total balls, 20 high-pressure balls, high-pressure participation in >=4 matches, median batting position <=7, and defined pressure average.

## Bowler eligibility
At least 60 total legal balls, 24 high-pressure legal balls, and high-pressure participation in >=4 matches.

## Ranking scores
Batter composite: pressure strike rate 25%, pressure average 25%, boundary rate 15%, inverse dot rate 10%, strike-rate retention 25%; within-pool percentiles shrunk toward 50 by n/(n+60).
Bowler composite: contextual runs saved per six legal balls 40%, bowler wickets per 100 pressure legal balls 25%, dot-ball rate 20%, inverse pressure economy 15%; within-pool percentiles shrunk toward 50 by n/(n+60).

## Critical caution
These are exploratory rankings, not validated final selections. All top-10 bowlers in the current shortlist have negative runs-saved-per-six values, meaning they conceded more than the empirical expected baseline in the pressure subset. A higher composite rank here means best relative among the screened candidate pool, not objectively good pressure performance. Before submission, review wicket attribution, source definitions for extras, and pressure classification. The data supports a useful comparative shortlist, but no claim that the top candidates are definitively the best clutch players should be made yet.
